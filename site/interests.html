Interests

Understanding how models learn

I’m interested in training dynamics, optimization, and mechanistic interpretability: how representations and capabilities develop, and whether understanding them can help us build more capable systems with less compute.

My current direction is computationally efficient interpretability. I want to explore tools that remain useful as a model’s weights change, and whether they can inform training, inference, distillation, and continual learning. Mechinterp Can Push the Pareto Frontier lays out the intuition, including questions about uncertainty and how increasingly capable models might work together. It’s an exploration of what could be useful, with plenty left to test.

I’m also interested in what our measurements actually establish. In The Fitting Corpus Is Part of the Measurement, I examine how changing the fitting corpus changes a Jacobian lens’s readouts. That question extends to evaluation, ablation studies, and reproducibility: how much of a result comes from the model, and how much comes from the tools and assumptions we use to study it?

Building to understand

I like working through the machinery myself. That has meant implementing attention, tokenization, and backpropagation from first principles, training AureliusGPT on Meditations, and building a PASCAL-to-MIPS compiler. There’s something satisfying about following an idea all the way down to an implementation.

I’m interested in the infrastructure around research, too: keeping experiments reproducible, tracking compute, and making literature search and evaluation less cumbersome. My projects include prototypes for compute preregistration and research workflows. I also care about turning useful ideas into software people can use, from LLM memory and task extraction to patient advocacy.

Intelligence, uncertainty, and people

Philosophy connects many of these interests. What does it mean for a system to understand something? How should it respond when it cannot be sure? And what should we ask of systems that increasingly shape how people think and make decisions?

My John Locke essay approaches alignment through the distinction between polite behavior and underlying principles. Conversations about AI and critical thinking, and interviews with people building these systems, give me other ways to explore those questions.
