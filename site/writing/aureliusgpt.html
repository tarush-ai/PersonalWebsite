Overview

I built AureliusGPT to work through language-model training using a text I enjoy: Marcus Aurelius’s Meditations. There are two implementations. The NumPy version has a handwritten BPE tokenizer and manual backpropagation. AureliusGPT-Torch uses PyTorch autograd and SentencePiece. The specifications above describe the PyTorch version.

Data

Project Gutenberg’s edition of Meditations provides approximately 89K tokens with a 2,000-token SentencePiece vocabulary. I supplemented it with roughly 122K tokens of synthetic text generated by a Llama 3.2 1B model fine-tuned on the book.

The student trains on generated text, not teacher logits. The teacher fine-tuning and generation ran on a Colab T4; student training ran on a local CPU.

Preprocessing includes Unicode normalization, Greek transliterations, and sequence boundary tokens. The training split contains 80% of the original text and the synthetic data, with the original training text included twice. The remaining 20% of the original text is used for validation.

Architecture

The PyTorch model has six PostNorm transformer blocks, causal multihead attention, learned token embeddings, and sinusoidal positional embeddings. Layer normalization and automatic differentiation use PyTorch’s implementations.

token embeddings + positional embeddings
  → 6 × [causal attention → add / norm → feedforward → add / norm]
  → vocabulary logits

Training & inference

I tracked training and validation loss to examine overfitting. Repeated passes over this small corpus can lower training loss without improving generation. The published model includes an epoch-10 checkpoint and tokenizer.

The demo accepts a short prompt and samples a continuation. Its optional GPT-4o commentary is separate from the model and is not a validated evaluation of generation quality.

Limitations

  • The corpus is small and heavily reused. Generated text can be repetitive, incoherent, or overly close to the training material.
  • The teacher prompts did not consistently constrain generation to Meditations, so synthetic examples can include other material or invented claims.
  • The teacher was fine-tuned on the source book. Holding out part of that book from the student’s direct training data alone does not establish an independent evaluation.

Possible follow-up work includes revising the synthetic-data prompts and evaluating checkpoints with a more carefully separated test set.
